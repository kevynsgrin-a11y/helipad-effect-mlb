Methodology Overview

This page explains the thinking behind our Helipad Index and long‑ball environment grades. The index synthesises key physical variables — air density, wind direction and magnitude, park orientation and dimensions, roof status and estimated pitcher stress — into a single 0‑100 score that rates how favourable conditions are for hitters. A higher score implies a more hitter‑friendly environment.

At this stage the model uses heuristic weightings inspired by published research. Air density is derived from temperature, humidity and barometric pressure; wind direction is resolved relative to centre field; and each park’s baseline run factor and sensitivity to wind modify the result. You can find a technical overview in the Model Notes.

Future iterations will incorporate statistically trained coefficients using historical game data and will account for humidor era adjustments, pitcher and lineup tendencies and rolling season validation. See the roadmap for more details.

Data Sources

Our data comes from publicly available sources, including weather forecasts, ballpark information, MLB game schedules and historical park factors. We also reference research on how temperature and wind affect carry distance.

Model Limitations

These analytics are for informational purposes only and are not intended to be gambling or betting advice. The model does not account for every possible factor (e.g., player‑specific performance, ballpark altitude changes or unreported injuries) and cannot guarantee outcomes. Always consider other variables and use your own judgment.
